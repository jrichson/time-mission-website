# Time Mission — Design Context

This file is the durable design brief for contributors working on the Time Mission website. Concrete tokens live in `css/variables.css`; this document captures the *intent* behind those tokens.

## Design Context

### Users

Time Mission is a **physical immersive social-gaming venue** with multiple US locations + Antwerp. The public website's job is to convert three overlapping audiences:

- **Friend groups (20s–30s)** browsing on mobile, deciding where to spend a Friday night. Want to be sold on the experience emotionally before they care about logistics.
- **Birthday party planners (parents)** booking for kids/teens. Need confidence the venue is professional, safe, and worth the price.
- **Corporate event buyers** evaluating team-building options. Need to see polish, social proof, and frictionless group booking.
- **Internal CMS editors and operators** who are non-technical or semi-technical. They need to update pages, landing pages, SEO metadata, invitations, publishing state, and previews without asking a developer for routine content work.

Public job to be done: **decide that "Time Mission" is the right kind of fun for this specific occasion** and book it without leaving the site.

CMS job to be done: **make the right content change confidently, preview it, publish it, and understand whether it affects the public site** without knowing Astro, Payload, Cloudflare, Railway, or deployment internals.

### Brand Personality

**Energetic, playful, social** — but delivered through a premium sci-fi venue aesthetic, not bright cartoon party energy. The look is dark and cinematic; the *feeling* is "let's gather a crew and do this."

Voice cues:
- Verb-first CTAs ("Book Now", "Visit Philadelphia", not "Submit" / "Click here")
- Confident, not corporate ("Your next mission" / "Choose your crew")
- Playful asides allowed (the rotating taglines like "Better Than Your Ex's Personality" / "Touch Grass... Indoors") — keeps the dark sci-fi from tipping into dark-and-serious

### Aesthetic Direction

**Dark by definition.** No light mode planned; the brand reads as "stepping into a mission control bunker." All design decisions are dark-first.

| Token category | Value | Notes |
|----------------|-------|-------|
| Primary | `--orange` `#FF6B2C` | Single confident accent. Use for CTAs and brand moments. |
| Backdrop | `--black` `#0D0D0D`, `--dark` `#151515` | Near-black, never pure `#000`. |
| Secondary accents | `--cyan` `#00E5FF`, `--magenta` `#FF00E5`, `--lime` `#AAFF00` | Available, but **use sparingly** — these are not equal-weight; they punctuate, they don't dominate. |
| Display type | Bebas Neue (`--display`) | Tall condensed sans for hero/section titles. |
| Tech accents | Monument Extended → Orbitron (`--tech`) | The "mission control" voice. Use for badges, metrics, ticker, eyebrow text. Don't apply to body copy. |
| Body | DM Sans (`--body`) | Workhorse readable sans. |
| Texture | Subtle SVG noise overlay (`opacity: 0.03`) + 80px grid lines on dark sections | Adds depth without competing for attention. |
| Spacing | 8 px base; clamped section spacing (`--space-section-y` etc.) | Generous breathing room above the fold; tighter rhythm in conversion sections. |

**First-impression emotion arc** (the homepage should deliver these in order, top to bottom):

1. **Excitement** — above the fold. Hero video + bold display type + animated tagline rotator. "I want to book this NOW."
2. **Curiosity** — missions / experiences carousel. Show the immersive worlds in motion; let the photography sell the wow. "Wait, what IS this?"
3. **Confidence** — testimonials + press logos + FAQ + booking. Social proof and polish reassure the planner-personas. "This place is the real deal."

**CMS/admin direction**:

The CMS should feel like a **Time Mission operator console**, not a cinematic marketing page. Keep the dark, gridded, orange-accented brand surface, but prioritize clarity, scanability, and low cognitive load over spectacle.

- Treat non-technical editors as the primary CMS users. Use plain labels, examples, descriptions, validation, and sensible defaults so they do not need to understand implementation details.
- Preserve Payload's familiar admin patterns where they help usability. Brand the shell, forms, previews, and key empty states; do not fight Payload so hard that the admin becomes harder to use.
- Make content scope obvious: "Existing Pages" edits metadata/content for canonical site pages; "Landing Pages" creates `/c/{slug}` campaign pages; "User Invites" controls access.
- Every content workflow should expose the next action: edit, save, preview, publish, invite, copy invite link, or deploy manually when applicable.
- Previews are part of the CMS contract. Editors should be able to see how a landing page will look before relying on a public deploy.
- Avoid developer language in editor-facing UI. Prefer "Page URL" over "slug" where possible, "Public preview" over "rendered route", and "Published" over "included in static generation" unless technical detail is truly needed.
- Use forms that are hard to misuse: associated labels, required indicators, inline help, field-level errors, loading/submission states, disabled states that look disabled, and success/error feedback that is not color-only.
- Keep all admin targets at least 44x44 px, preserve visible keyboard focus, and ensure body text remains 16 px or larger on small screens.
- Use orange for primary action and status emphasis; cyan can label system/context hints. Do not use the full accent palette at once inside CMS screens.

**Gradient policy**:

The client likes Time Mission's gradients, so gradients are an intentional brand asset on public marketing surfaces. Keep them controlled: prefer the existing orange/gold `--gradient-primary` and `--gradient-accent` families for high-energy brand moments, CTAs, and section emphasis. Avoid saturated rainbow gradients, generic SaaS blue/purple gradients, and gradient text inside CMS/editor product UI. CMS screens may use subtle environmental gradients for depth, but editing clarity always wins over visual spectacle.

### Anti-References

What this brand should explicitly **NOT** look like:

- ❌ **Generic family-fun (Chuck E. Cheese, Dave & Buster's)** — no bright primary colors, no cartoon mascots, no kids'-birthday-party energy. The venue is for groups of all ages, but the *visual* aims at the 20–30s segment first; families self-select in.
- ❌ **Indie escape room (DIY, low-budget)** — no amateur photography, no cluttered layouts, no lo-fi typography. Production values must signal "real venue, real investment."
- ❌ **VR arcade chrome (rainbow gradients on neon)** — no saturated rainbow gradients, no heavy glow effects, no "every VR startup ever" look. The accent palette exists; it does not get used as a rainbow.
- ❌ **Corporate SaaS (rounded blue gradients, hero illustrations)** — no startup landing-page aesthetic. Time Mission is a physical place, not a B2B tool. Imagery is photography of the actual venues; not abstract illustration.

### Design Principles

These should guide every design decision:

1. **One strong color moment per section.** The accent palette is wide (orange, cyan, magenta, lime, gold) but each section earns one accent, not all of them. Orange is the default; everything else is a deliberate exception.
2. **Photography over illustration.** Real venue photos and game-experience imagery do the heavy lifting. Illustrations and abstract gradients are not part of this brand.
3. **Dark surfaces, intentional texture.** Pure flat black reads cheap; pure heavy texture reads chaotic. The combination of `#0D0D0D` near-black + 3% noise overlay + 80px grid lines is the surface contract.
4. **Type carries hierarchy, not size alone.** Bebas Neue (display) ↔ Monument Extended/Orbitron (tech) ↔ DM Sans (body) is the three-voice contrast that makes layouts feel intentional. Don't mix more fonts in.
5. **Verb-first CTAs, primary/secondary/tertiary discipline.** Each section has one primary action. Secondary is outline-only. Tertiary is a text link. Equal-weight buttons are a smell.
6. **Visual parity is the contract.** This site is a parity-first migration from a hand-authored predecessor; visual changes need explicit justification, not drift.
7. **Reduced motion respected.** 3D tilt, autoplay video, and parallax effects all check `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` and degrade gracefully.
8. **CMS usability beats CMS decoration.** The admin should borrow the brand's surface language, but every styling choice must make editing, previewing, publishing, and inviting easier for non-technical users.
9. **Guide editors through structure.** Prefer templates, constrained choices, field descriptions, previews, and validation over free-form fields that require institutional knowledge.
10. **Status must be legible.** Draft/published state, preview availability, invite status, deploy behavior, and errors must be visible in words, not color alone.
11. **Routine content work should not require code.** When a page type or campaign pattern repeats, the CMS should absorb that workflow through structured content and previewable templates instead of creating future developer tickets.
12. **Accessibility is part of polish.** Maintain contrast, labels, focus states, keyboard order, reduced motion, and mobile tap targets in both public pages and CMS customizations.

### Reference files

- `css/variables.css` — concrete design tokens (canonical)
- `cms/app/(payload)/custom.scss` — Payload admin brand layer; keep it operator-console clear and consistent with public tokens
- `cms/collections/` — editor-facing content models; labels, descriptions, validation, and preview affordances must be treated as UX
- `cms/app/preview/landings/[id]/` — CMS landing-preview surface; align it with public site rhythm while making draft review obvious
- `docs/DESIGN-DOCUMENTATION.md` — historical prototype comparison (Energetic & Playful was the chosen direction)
- `assets/logo/` — TM logos and favicon
- `assets/photos/` — venue and experience photography (the brand's primary visual surface)
- `js/page-widgets.js` — shared widget vocabulary (typewriter, carousel, ticker, tilt, etc.) — use these primitives instead of authoring new ones inline per page
